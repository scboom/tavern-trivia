# Obscure Trivia

Free game quizzes for the details only real players know: https://scboom.github.io/obscure-trivia/

Each quiz and game has its own themed page, a bank of 1,000 questions, 20 random questions per game, solo play, a 10-minute time limit, party mode for 2 to 6 players with a 5-letter code (host starts the quiz; answers revealed together at the end, skipped only if every player votes, then a ranking; afterwards the host can "Play again" or "Change quiz" and the whole party moves with them), a small "Top scores" tab with the 3 best scores of the last 7 days (every player in a party counts, and one person can hold all three; ties go to the faster time), and a "Recent quizzes" list of your last 5 games.

## How it's built

- `index.html` is the hub. Each quiz folder has its page and `questions.json`.
- `assets/quiz.js` and `assets/quiz.css` are the shared quiz engine and layout; each quiz page sets its own theme and settings.
- `locked/` is a password-protected test area for trying things before they go live (Blackjack started there). Its contents are encrypted in the page (AES-256-GCM, with the key made from the password by PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 rounds) by `tools/locked.js`, which can also decrypt it again for editing. The password is needed every time the page opens: nothing is remembered. The password isn't stored anywhere in this repository.
- Spend coins (`spend-coins/`, a small link at the bottom of the home page, next to the locked area, and in every coin pop-up): Blackjack for everyone. You type your bet or use ½, ×2 and All; cards fly out of the shoe and turn over, with card, chip and table sounds made in the browser (they can be muted). Signed in to an account, it's played entirely on the server (`tools/accounts.sql`): the server shuffles a fresh 6-deck shoe for every hand, keeps the shoe and the dealer's face-down card secret, checks every move against the real hand and pays out itself, so the browser can't see, change or fake anything. Without an account, the same rules run in the browser and bet the coins saved in it (a hand in progress is saved, so a reload carries on with it). Rules: dealer peeks and stands on all 17s, blackjack pays 3 to 2, other wins 1 to 1, double (also after splitting), split to 4 hands, insurance. The server's game was checked step by step against the browser engine, which a 30-million-hand simulation shows has the expected 0.45% house edge. Sources are in `tools/src/spend/`.
- Coins: 1 for every right answer, in every quiz. Without an account they're kept in the browser. With an account (a username and password on a Supabase database set up by `tools/accounts.sql`) the server is the only thing that can change them: a right answer is claimed once per question of each game, no faster than a quick player can answer, and blackjack pays out on the server; there is no way to add coins otherwise. A new account brings up to 500 of the browser's coins along. Passwords are bcrypt hashes, sessions are hashed tokens, and 10 wrong passwords lock a name for 15 minutes. Accounts are switched on by building with the project's address and public key in `ACCOUNTS_URL` and `ACCOUNTS_KEY`, or in `tools/src/accounts.json`.
- `tools/` holds the generators: `gen.py` (Hearthstone, from the game's card data), `gen_mc.py` (Minecraft, from Bedrock game data plus hand-checked facts), `gen_world.py` (General Knowledge: country data from mledoze/countries, ODbL, flags from flag-icons, MIT, plus hand-written fun facts), `earth.js` (draws the Earth pictures from Natural Earth map data), `pl_parse.py` + `gen_football.py` (Football: Premier League and World Cup results from openfootball, public domain, plus hand-written questions), `gen_cars.py` (Cars: makers' horsepower, top speed and rough UK price figures, each with a margin so only clear-cut comparisons are asked, plus hand-written questions), `gen_music.py` (Music: US chart questions worked out from every Billboard Hot 100 since 1958, from utdata/rwd-billboard-data, plus hand-written songs, albums, artists, records, Eurovision winners and film songs; no lyrics), `fn_extract.py` + `gen_fortnite.py` (OG Fortnite, Chapter 1 only, from the Pre-Season to Season X, with nothing later in the questions, wrong answers or explanations: outfits and the season they arrived in, from Fortnite-API's cosmetics list; seasons, named locations and live events, from the Fortnite Wiki (CC BY-SA); plus hand-written questions on the big moments of each season, items, Battle Passes, the map and history, checked against the wiki; map questions count the main Battle Royale island only), `cr_extract.py` + `gen_royale.py` (Clash Royale: card costs, rarities, types, unlock arenas, release dates, in-game descriptions, stat tables, Evolutions, Heroes and Champion abilities from the Clash Royale Wiki (CC BY-SA), as of November 2026; a stat is only asked about when it matches the latest change in that card's History, and hitpoint and damage comparisons need a clear 25% gap; plus hand-written mechanics and game facts checked against the same wiki), `lua_tables.py` + `dbd_fetch.py` + `dbd_pages.py` + `dbd_perks.py` + `gen_dbd.py` (Dead by Daylight, from the official Dead by Daylight Wiki at deadbydaylight.wiki.gg (CC BY-SA), as of the Terrifier Chapter, November 2026: the wiki's data tables for Killers, Survivors, perks, powers, maps, realms, Chapters, add-ons and offerings, read as plain data without running them, the Perks page for descriptions and quotes, and every character page; plus look-alike surname spellings and made-up Killer titles written by hand (`tools/src/dbd/names.json`), and lore and game-fact questions written from the wiki (`tools/src/dbd/lore_q.json`, `facts_q.json`), each carrying the exact wiki text behind it, which the generator checks again), `animals_extract.py` + `animals_check.py` + `wd_query.py` + `gen_animals.py` (Animals, from English Wikipedia (CC BY-SA): the List of animal names, the List of national animals and each animal's taxonomy box for its class and IUCN status, plus dog and cat breed origins from Wikidata (CC0); records, bodies, behaviour, habitats and diets were written from Wikipedia articles, each with the exact sentence behind it (`tools/src/animals/facts_q.json`), checked again by the generator), `gen_odd.py` (Odd One Out: tables of things and their features, from the site's own checked data, Wikidata countries, the Animals table and hand-checked general-knowledge tables in `tools/src/odd/tables.json`; a round is only kept if no other known feature, nor the first letter or number of words, would single out a different one of the four), `gen_timeline.py` (Timeline: dated events from English Wikipedia, each with the sentence that gives its date (`tools/src/timeline/events.json`), plus Clash Royale, Dead by Daylight and football dates from this site's own data; events are only compared when their dates are clearly apart), `gen_bigger.py` (This or That, first called Which Is Bigger?: country populations from the World Bank via datasets/population, CC BY 4.0, country areas from world-countries, Premier League seasons from openfootball, plus hand-entered published figures, each with an uncertainty margin so only clear-cut pairs are used) and `build_site.py`, which builds the pages.

The Battlegrounds emblem's crown shape comes from HearthSim's hs-icons (CC0).
The Clash Royale tile's crown and Elixir drop are original artwork (`tools/src/cr-crown.svg`).
The Dead by Daylight hook is original artwork (`tools/src/dbd-hook.svg`), and so are the Animals paw, the Odd One Out shapes and the Timeline hourglass (`tools/src/an-paw.svg`, `odd-icon.svg`, `tl-icon.svg`).
The Fortnite tile's Peely picture comes from Fortnite-API's cosmetics images (© Epic Games).
The Minecraft tile's sheep is rendered from Mojang's own sheep model and texture (Mojang/bedrock-samples, © Mojang AB) by `tools/render-sheep/`.
